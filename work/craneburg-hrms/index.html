Human Resource & Payroll Management

Bringing employee records, attendance, approvals, and payroll into one connected workflow.

Craneburg Construction
Role
UI/UX Designer
Duration
4 months
OverviewProblemGoalResearchDesign processTestingSolutionResultsReflection

01 · OVERVIEW

Bringing fragmented HR operations into one connected system.

Craneburg Construction needed to simplify workforce management across its offices and construction sites in Nigeria. Paper records, spreadsheets, and disconnected processes made everyday HR tasks time-consuming and difficult to coordinate.

I worked within a cross-functional team to design an HRMS covering employee onboarding, personnel records, attendance, requests, approvals, and payroll. The challenge was to connect these activities in a way that reflected how site teams, project managers, and HR staff actually worked.

My role
UI/UX Designer
Collaborators
Two designers working with product, business analysis, engineering, and QA.
Project timeline
4 months

Some project information has been adjusted or withheld for client confidentiality.

02 · PROBLEM

Manual processes were making workforce management harder to control.

The project context described a workforce of more than 3,000 employees and sites processing attendance for an average of 280 employees per day. At that scale, consolidating manual records created a substantial administrative workload.

01

Manual attendance collation

Managers consolidated register entries covering clock-in, breaks, clock-out, and overtime before further processing.

02

Scattered employee information

Paper files and spreadsheets made personnel updates, transfers, and terminations difficult to coordinate.

03

High-volume onboarding

A new site could require onboarding 100–250 contract personnel and establishing their records and work placements.

04

Complicated payroll preparation

Repeated checks of attendance and salary inputs prolonged calculations and contributed to payment delays.

03 · GOAL

Make workforce information dependable and HR tasks easier to complete.

The business needed to reduce administrative effort and improve workforce oversight. Users needed a clearer way to record attendance, maintain personnel information, submit requests, and prepare payroll.

The design goal was to connect these tasks through understandable navigation, clear actions, and access appropriate to each role.

Create one dependable workflow from employee onboarding and attendance to approvals and payroll.

04 · RESEARCH

Understanding the people behind every record and approval.

The Double Diamond and Lean UX approaches guided the work, supported by the 5W1H framework. Stakeholder conversations, user interviews, and market reviews helped clarify why the system was needed, who would use it, and where work happened.

Research pointed to decentralised data as a central problem. Site administrators needed faster attendance processing; project managers needed team visibility and clearer approvals; HR managers needed reliable employee records and payroll inputs. These needs informed the role-based approach.

05 · DESIGN PROCESS

Turning organisational complexity into connected workflows.

Research informed personas, empathy maps, task flows, and information architecture. Working with product owners, we clarified the project vision and used a decision matrix to support feature prioritisation.

Sketches and wireframes explored structure before detailed interface design. A visual system established typography, colour, icons, and reusable components across web and mobile experiences.

The overall project lasted four months, with an initial design window described as three weeks. Changing requirements and limited UX writing support required careful prioritisation. I also helped developers become familiar with Figma for handoff.

Employee lifecycle

Connect onboarding, personnel updates, attendance, and payroll inputs.

Role-based access

Organise information and actions around site, project, and HR responsibilities.

Consistent interfaces

Use shared components and clear microcopy across repeated tasks.

06 · TESTING

Walking through realistic tasks with the people using the system.

Usability sessions with primary users explored scenarios and actions to understand whether the workflows addressed their tasks and pain points. We used feedback to iterate on the designs.

The reviews connected interface decisions to familiar working practices, including paper forms and attendance registers. The emphasis was on task clarity and the fit between the system and daily operations.

07 · SOLUTION

One platform for employee information, attendance, and payroll.

The HRMS brought employee data into a central system so authorised teams could enter, update, and review workforce information. Structured onboarding supported personnel records, while attendance and timesheets connected daily activity to payroll preparation.

Requests and approvals linked operational tasks to the people responsible for reviewing them. A companion attendance-tracking application extended the solution to attendance capture, with the aim of reducing manual entry errors and inconsistent records.

08 · RESULTS

A clearer foundation for managing people across sites.

The project feedback describes improvements in attendance tracking and payroll administration. The strongest supported outcome is the move from scattered manual records toward a more connected HR workflow.

The available outcome evidence is qualitative. Measuring payroll preparation time, attendance corrections, and onboarding completion time would provide a clearer assessment of impact.

09 · REFLECTION

Complex systems become usable when they reflect real responsibilities.

This project deepened my understanding of enterprise design and role-based access. I learned to return to the underlying problem when interface details became distracting, and to treat the connections between people, tasks, and information as part of the design itself.

Design the workflow between screens as carefully as the screens themselves.
