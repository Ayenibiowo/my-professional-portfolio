Source to Settle Procurement & Inventory

Connecting procurement requests, approvals, vendors, budgets, and inventory across a branch network.

01 · OVERVIEW

Replacing fragmented operations with one connected system.

Keystone Bank needed a procurement and inventory platform that could connect work across its branch network. Vendor information, purchase requests, approvals, and expense records were difficult to coordinate through manual tools and disconnected processes.

I worked with the cross-functional team to shape a Source to Settle experience covering requests, vendor management, inventory, budgets, and financial reconciliation. The design needed to preserve the bank’s approval hierarchy while making day-to-day work easier to follow.

My role
UI/UX Designer
Collaborators

02 · PROBLEM

Scale had turned everyday procurement into an operational risk.

Manual procurement processes created delays and errors, while scattered records limited visibility into vendors, inventory, and expenditure. Without a central view, teams found it difficult to reconcile payments and maintain a dependable record of capital and operational spending.

01

Slow request processing

Manual hand-offs made it harder to follow progress and coordinate approval across teams.

02

Fragmented vendor information

Quotes and vendor communications arrived through separate emails, limiting consistent comparison and documentation.

03

Limited stock visibility

Teams needed more precise inventory information to reduce stock shortages and unnecessary purchasing.

04

Weak financial traceability

Disconnected payments, expense records, and approvals complicated reconciliation and audit preparation.

03 · GOAL

Make procurement visible, controlled, and easier to complete.

The product needed to consolidate vendor management, purchase processing, reconciliation, and inventory into a connected workflow. Budget visibility and a clear approval history were central to the experience.

The source sets inventory accuracy and budget-adherence ambitions for the first year. Those figures are goals, not verified post-launch results.

Create a dependable operating system for requests, approvals, vendors, budgets, and inventory.

04 · RESEARCH

Understanding the people behind each request and budget decision.

Discovery calls and requirements workshops brought together stakeholders from finance, administration, operations, facilities, technology, legal, HR, marketing, and logistics. We examined responsibilities, current pain points, and the information needed at each stage.

User interviews and market research helped inform feature priorities and familiar patterns. Role definitions distinguished procurement teams handling supplies, finance teams reconciling expenditure, and financial controllers needing oversight and secure approvals.

The central insight was that each role needed a different level of detail, but everyone depended on a consistent account of the request, its budget, and its progress.

05 · DESIGN PROCESS

Moving from organisational complexity to a coherent product model.

Working with application owners, we clarified essential features and mapped the information architecture across procurement and inventory activities. User flows connected the lifecycle from a request to its approval and financial handling.

Sketches and wireframes established structure before the detailed interfaces. The design used shared visual patterns for requests, dashboards, budgets, and approval information.

The initial design schedule was less than a month, with web and mobile requirements in the same window. The team balanced those constraints with evolving stakeholder needs and the work of writing interface copy.

06 · TESTING

Testing whether people could follow the work across roles.

User reviews explored navigation, task completion, and the clarity of the procurement experience. The source records positive feedback on ease of use and observations of fewer follow-up questions about approvals.

Testing also suggested opportunities to improve procurement cycle time. This remained an opportunity for post-launch measurement, rather than an established operational result.

07 · SOLUTION

One platform for requests, approvals, vendors, and inventory.

The platform brought request creation, approval progress, and budget information into a connected experience. Dashboard views gave users a way to review activity alongside capital and operational expenditure summaries.

The request workflow connected what was being requested, why it was needed, and the available financial context. The approval experience made the organisational chain part of the product rather than a separate email conversation.

Vendor, inventory, and financial-management capabilities formed the wider Source to Settle scope, supporting a more consistent operational record.

08 · RESULTS

Clearer ownership and a stronger basis for operational decisions.

User feedback described an easier-to-navigate interface and clearer approval visibility. The design established a connected model for procurement, inventory, and expenditure across the organisation.

The next evaluation should compare request turnaround, inventory accuracy, budget variance, and the frequency of approval-status enquiries. This would separate usability feedback from measurable operational impact.

09 · REFLECTION

Complex systems become usable when responsibility is visible.

This project deepened how I think about enterprise software. Connecting modules was only part of the work: each role also needed to understand what had happened, what required attention, and who should act next. Clear responsibility became a useful principle for navigating that complexity.

A clear enterprise interface makes responsibility, progress, and exceptions easier to understand.
