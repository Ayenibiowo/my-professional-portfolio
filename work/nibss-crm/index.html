Customer Relationship Management

Connecting customer information, lead management, campaigns, and sales visibility in one workspace.

01 · OVERVIEW

Turning scattered customer information into a connected sales journey.

NIBSS needed a CRM to manage customer interactions and make sales activity easier to coordinate. Relationship managers and business-development teams worked across spreadsheets, email, and phone conversations, making a complete picture of the customer difficult to maintain.

I worked with designers, the project manager, and business analysts to understand the needs of the participating teams and translate them into a coherent customer-management experience. The scope connected leads, opportunities, campaigns, communications, and reporting.

My role
UI/UX Designer
Collaborators
Three designers working with product, business analysis, engineering, and QA.
Project timeline
5 months

Some project information has been adjusted or withheld for client confidentiality.

02 · PROBLEM

Customer activity was visible in fragments, but difficult to follow as a whole.

The organisation collected leads through several channels, but information could become outdated or remain with individual team members. It was difficult to establish the current stage of an opportunity or relate sales effort to completed deals.

01

Fragmented follow-up

Teams copied contacts from spreadsheets to phones and searched separate email conversations to continue engagement.

02

Unclear opportunity status

There was no dependable central view linking a lead to customer needs, product opportunities, and its current stage.

03

Repeated data consolidation

Teams merged spreadsheets to obtain the latest customer information, creating extra work and inconsistent records.

04

Limited performance visibility

Campaign and sales activity were difficult to compare with outcomes, limiting the insight available for decisions.

03 · GOAL

Make each customer relationship easier to understand and move forward.

The goal was a unified CRM that consolidated customer information and simplified the lead-to-deal process. Relationship managers needed continuity in customer conversations, while leadership needed a clearer view of activity and performance.

The source identified improved prospect conversion as a business ambition. It is treated as a target rather than a demonstrated result.

Connect the customer, the conversation, and the next opportunity in one dependable workspace.

04 · RESEARCH

Learning how different teams contribute to the customer lifecycle.

Discovery calls and workshops involved relationship management, product, operations, technology, and executive stakeholders. Designers, the project manager, and business analysts worked together to clarify their responsibilities and pain points.

Interviews and market research helped identify familiar conventions and prioritise features. The findings pointed toward combining the sales cycle in one place: lead information, campaigns, closed deals, sales activity, and reports.

Different roles needed different views of the same customer journey. Business developers focused on opportunities, relationship managers on engagement, and leadership on progress and performance.

05 · DESIGN PROCESS

Organising the experience around the journey from lead to relationship.

The team worked with product owners to align on a clear vision and essential features. Information architecture and user flows connected activities across the sales cycle instead of treating customer records as isolated entries.

Sketches, mid-fidelity wireframes, and detailed interface designs translated the structure into screens. A consistent component language supported lead lists, detail panels, campaigns, and reporting.

The work balanced an initial design schedule of less than a month with web and mobile requirements and limited UX writing support.

06 · TESTING

Reviewing the workflow with its intended users.

The source describes reviewing the application with users to gather feedback after design. The focus was on the experience of managing customer information and sales activity.

The available record does not contain a task-by-task issue log, participant counts, or a complete account of the resulting iterations. No additional test findings or preference figures are inferred.

07 · SOLUTION

One workspace for leads, campaigns, and relationship context.

The CRM connected customer records with lead tracking, campaign activity, and reporting. A lead list and contextual detail panel helped bring information together within the same working view.

Campaign screens connected campaign details with performance information, supporting a more coherent review of engagement activity. The broader product direction supported sales coordination and reduced dependence on merging separate spreadsheets.

08 · RESULTS

A shared customer record and a clearer view of sales activity.

The draft reports that the solution reduced the need to merge spreadsheets and helped automate sales processes. These are described as qualitative project outcomes.

Future evaluation should track lead-to-deal conversion, follow-up completion, record completeness, and time spent consolidating customer information. These measures would give a clearer assessment of business impact.

09 · REFLECTION

A CRM should help people continue the relationship.

The design challenge was more than collecting customer data. The useful product connects what the team knows, what has already happened, and the next action needed to move the relationship forward. Designing across team boundaries was essential to that continuity.

Customer information becomes useful when it helps someone take the next meaningful action.
