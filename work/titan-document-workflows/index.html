Document Management & Approval Workflows

Making documents easier to create, retrieve, and move through approval with clear ownership.

Client
Titan Trust Bank
Role
Lead Designer
Duration
4 months
OverviewProblemGoalResearchDesign processTestingSolutionResultsReflection

01 · OVERVIEW

Replacing disconnected document handling with traceable workflows.

Titan Trust Bank needed a web-based platform to create and manage documents from different sources and coordinate review and approval. Paper archives and dispersed digital files made retrieval difficult, while approval delays slowed everyday work.

As lead designer, I was involved in stakeholder engagement, research, UX, UI, and interaction design. The work needed to accommodate multiple approval levels and support the bank’s evolving organisational needs.

Lead Designer

02 · PROBLEM

Finding the document was only the beginning of the work.

Employees navigated paper archives, folders, emails, and other systems to locate business documents. Even when a document was found, moving it through the right review and approval steps could take days. Research described memo approvals taking between one and seven days.

01

Slow approvals

Employees depended on department heads and management decisions without a sufficiently clear view of progress.

02

Difficult retrieval

Large physical archives and scattered digital files made locating the right document a recurring bottleneck.

03

Disconnected information

Critical documents lived across folders, email accounts, and separate tools, limiting collaboration.

04

Tool and growth constraints

The bank needed a workflow tailored to its approval structure and security requirements while addressing the costs of external tools.

03 · GOAL

Make document work easier to follow from creation to decision.

The goal was an understandable web interface that reduced manual document handling and supported an automated approval workflow.

The product needed to connect document creation, retrieval, and review while making the status and next action visible to both employees and approvers.

Help employees find the right document and move it to the right person with its context intact.

04 · RESEARCH

Understanding both sides of the approval process.

Desk research, employee interviews, contextual inquiries, and surveys explored document handling and approval workflows. We examined frustrations, needs, motivations, and the existing journey.

Affinity mapping helped organise qualitative findings into themes. Two core personas — a regular employee and an approver — kept attention on the different needs involved in submitting and reviewing work.

The central learning was that retrieval and approvals were connected problems: people needed the right information and a clear understanding of what happened next.

05 · DESIGN PROCESS

Building clarity into documents, tables, and approval journeys.

The team worked with application owners to define the product vision and prioritise features. High-level user flows and sketches established the structure before detailed design.

We used Titan Trust Bank’s visual identity and developed a style guide and component library for consistency. Table interactions, document previews, navigation, and search were treated as essential parts of task completion.

Scheduling stakeholders, working through initially biased feedback, and writing microcopy without a dedicated UX writer were important constraints.

Create

Use memo templates and a structured creation flow to reduce repeated setup.

Find

Support advanced search and useful document details across organisational units.

Review

Keep the approval journey understandable as documents move between people.

06 · TESTING

Using feedback to improve the information available at a glance.

Iterative usability sessions walked primary users through scenarios and actions. We compared memo-creation and file-listing alternatives to understand navigation and information needs.

Participants asked to see more document information without leaving their current view. The design direction therefore prioritised useful detail at a glance and familiar patterns for reviewing files.

The available feedback supports a qualitative account of the design decision: useful document details should be accessible without disrupting the current task.

07 · SOLUTION

A connected workspace for creating, finding, and approving documents.

The solution combined document creation and memo templates with retrieval tools and an approval workflow. Advanced search was designed to help employees locate documents across individuals, teams, units, and departments.

The interface used tables and contextual information to support everyday work, while an expandable navigation approach preserved room for the content. The approval flow addressed the delays identified during research.

08 · RESULTS

More consistent workflows and clearer document access.

The case study describes standardised document workflows and positive usability feedback. The design created a more coherent way to work with documents and approval steps across teams.

A stronger post-launch assessment would measure retrieval time, approval turnaround, overdue reviews, and repeated requests for status updates. Unverified cost-saving claims are not treated as measured outcomes.

09 · REFLECTION

A document system also needs to explain the decision around the document.

This work reinforced the importance of designing for both the person submitting a document and the person reviewing it. Useful context, familiar interactions, and visible next steps can turn a collection of files into a workflow people can understand.

The document matters. So do its status, its context, and the person responsible for the next step.
